Price From Margin:
Never Underprice Again

Stop guessing your price. Enter your cost and target margin — this formula calculates the exact minimum you need to charge to hit that number.

=CEILING(A2/(1-B2), 0.01)

A2 = cost  |  B2 = target margin as a decimal (e.g. 0.60 for 60%)

How It Works

1

1-B2 — converts your margin percentage into the cost fraction. If you want 60% margin, the cost must represent 40% of the price, so this gives you 0.40.

2

A2/(1-B2) — divides your cost by that fraction. This is the raw minimum price required to achieve exactly your target margin — not a penny over or under.

3

CEILING(..., 0.01) — rounds up to the nearest penny. This ensures you never accidentally underprice by a fraction of a cent. Always round up, never down.

Solopreneur

Pricing a Digital Product

You're launching an Excel template. Your time and tool costs work out to £12 per sale. You want a 70% margin minimum. This formula tells you the lowest price you can charge:

=CEILING(12/(1-0.70), 0.01)

Result: £40.00 minimum price — anything under that and you're missing your margin target.

Small Business

Product or Service Pricing

You're quoting a job or pricing a physical product with known costs. You want to protect a 45% gross margin across your product range. Set up a pricing calculator with one formula per row:

=CEILING(C2/(1-D2), 0.01)

Put your margin target in column D (e.g. 0.45) and costs in column C. Copy the formula down for every product line.

Pro Tips

  • → Use a named range for your target margin: Put your margin in a single cell (e.g. F1) and reference it as $F$1 — change the margin once and every product reprices automatically.
  • → Check actual margin against target: Add a column using =(selling_price-cost)/selling_price to see your real margin on any price you've set.
  • → Highlight underpriced items: Use conditional formatting on your selling price column — =D2<CEILING(A2/(1-B2),0.01) — to flag any product priced below its minimum.
  • → Model different margin scenarios: Create a small table with margins from 40% to 80% in steps of 5% — run the formula across each to see how your price needs to shift as your cost targets change.
