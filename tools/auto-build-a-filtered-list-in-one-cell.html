Excel Formula Tips

Auto-Build a Filtered List
in One Cell

Stop copying and pasting active items into your reports by hand. TEXTJOIN + IF does it automatically.

=TEXTJOIN(", ", TRUE, IF(B2:B10="Active", A2:A10, ""))

In Excel 365/2021: press Enter. Older Excel: Ctrl+Shift+Enter

How It Works

1

IF(B2:B10="Active", A2:A10, "") — scans each row and returns the name if the status matches, or a blank if it doesn't.

2

TEXTJOIN(", ", TRUE, …) — combines all non-blank results into one cell, separated by commas. The TRUE argument skips any blanks automatically.

3

Change the status word to match your data — "Done", "Paid", "In Progress", or whatever label you use. The formula adapts instantly.

Solopreneur

Active Client Summary

You've got a client tracker with 20 rows. Some are Active, some Paused, some Completed. Instead of filtering every time you want a quick list of who you're actively working with, put this in a summary cell:

=TEXTJOIN(", ", TRUE, IF(C2:C20="Active", A2:A20, ""))

Result: "Sarah's Bakery, TechStart Ltd, Jamie Freelance" — updates the moment you change a status.

Small Business

Weekly Task Rollup

Your team tracks tasks in a shared spreadsheet with a Status column. At your weekly review, you want a single-cell summary of everything still "In Progress" — without manually scanning 50 rows:

=TEXTJOIN(", ", TRUE, IF(D2:D50="In Progress", B2:B50, ""))

Drop this into your dashboard header. No filtering, no copying — the list builds itself.

Pro Tips

  • → Change the separator: Replace ", " with " | " or " / " for different display styles.
  • → Make the filter dynamic: Replace "Active" with a cell reference like E1 — then change E1 to switch what you're filtering instantly.
  • → Expand your range: Use a full column reference like B:B so the formula picks up new rows automatically as your list grows.
  • → Combine with COUNT: Pair with =COUNTIF(B2:B10,"Active") to show both the count and the names in your dashboard.
